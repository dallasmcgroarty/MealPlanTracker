Tier 3 — Unapproved Research Chemical

TB-500 is sold online labeled "not for human consumption," specifically to avoid FDA drug regulation. There is little to no human safety data. This page describes what's known — not dosing or usage guidance, and not a recommendation to use it. Talk to a doctor before considering it.

What It Is

TB-500 is a synthetic fragment/analog of thymosin beta-4, a protein naturally involved in cell structure and migration. Its proposed mechanism, based entirely on animal and cell-culture research, involves regulating actin (a structural protein) to promote cell movement and tissue repair — the basis for its marketing around injury recovery.

What Research Actually Exists

There are no completed human clinical trials of TB-500. What exists is preclinical — animal and cell studies looking at wound healing and tissue repair. There is essentially no human evidence for safety or effectiveness, which is different from "the human evidence is weak" — for TB-500 specifically, it doesn't meaningfully exist yet.

Current Legal & Regulatory Status

TB-500 has never been FDA-approved for any use. It is sold as a "research chemical," explicitly labeled not for human consumption — language sellers use specifically to operate outside FDA drug regulation. Its compounding-pharmacy status has shifted more than once in 2026: previously restricted from compounding (the FDA's "Category 2" list), that restriction was lifted in April 2026 when the nomination was withdrawn, and in July 2026 an FDA advisory committee narrowly recommended allowing it to be compounded going forward — a recommendation the FDA had not yet acted on as of this writing. None of this amounts to approval; it only affects whether a compounding pharmacy can legally prepare it. It's also prohibited at all times in competitive sport under the World Anti-Doping Agency's Prohibited List.

Known & Theoretical Risks

With no completed human trials, real risks in people — immune reactions, impurity from unregulated manufacturing, long-term effects — are unstudied rather than ruled out. Products sold as "TB-500" also carry the additional, practical risk of unverified purity and dosing consistency, since they aren't manufactured under pharmaceutical quality standards.
