BPC-157

Never approved, restricted for compounding, and banned in competitive sport.

Tier 2 — Investigational / Not Currently Approved

BPC-157 has some published research behind it, but it is not FDA-approved for any use, and human clinical trial data is extremely limited. This page describes what's actually known — not dosing or usage guidance, and not a recommendation to use it. Talk to a doctor before considering it.

What It Is

BPC-157 ("Body Protection Compound-157") is a synthetic 15-amino-acid peptide fragment, originally sequenced from a protective protein identified in human gastric juice. Proposed mechanisms — based on animal research — include promoting angiogenesis (new blood vessel formation), modulating nitric-oxide signaling, and reducing inflammation, which is why it's marketed around tissue and tendon repair.

What Research Actually Exists

The evidence base is overwhelmingly animal research — rat and mouse studies on gut, tendon, and wound healing. Human data is genuinely minimal: a small 2025 safety pilot gave an intravenous dose to just 2 healthy adults with no adverse effects reported, and a couple of other very small studies (roughly a dozen or so patients each) have looked at it for bladder pain and knee pain. There is no completed large-scale human clinical trial. This is a real research gap, not a technicality — claims of "proven safety" or "proven efficacy" in humans go well beyond what this evidence actually supports.

Current Legal & Regulatory Status

BPC-157 has never been FDA-approved for any human use. Its compounding status has shifted more than once in 2026: the FDA had restricted it from compounding pharmacies (its "Category 2" bulk substances list, citing safety-data and impurity concerns), but that restriction was lifted in April 2026 when the nomination was withdrawn. In July 2026, an FDA advisory committee met to consider formally adding it to the list of substances compounding pharmacies are allowed to use — but the FDA had not acted on that recommendation as of this writing. In short: no approval either way, and its compounding status remains unsettled and could change again.

It's also prohibited at all times in competitive sport under the World Anti-Doping Agency's Prohibited List.

Known & Theoretical Risks

Because robust human safety data doesn't exist, real risks — immune reactions, impurity-related harms, long-term effects — are essentially unstudied in people rather than ruled out. "Generally regarded as safe" marketing claims are not backed by the kind of human toxicology data that supports that phrase for an approved drug.
