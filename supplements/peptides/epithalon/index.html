Epithalon (Epitalon)

Telomerase and longevity claims trace almost entirely to one research group — not independently established.

← Back to Peptides

Tier 3 — Unapproved Research Chemical

Epithalon is sold online as a research chemical, is not FDA-approved for any use, and its core scientific claims come almost entirely from a single research group, not a broad independently-replicated evidence base. This page describes what's known — not dosing or usage guidance, and not a recommendation to use it. Talk to a doctor before considering it.

What It Is

Epithalon (also spelled Epitalon; research code AEDG, for its four constituent amino acids) is a synthetic tetrapeptide based on epithalamin, a pineal-gland extract studied for decades by Russian researcher Dr. Vladimir Khavinson and colleagues. Its headline claimed mechanism is activating telomerase, the enzyme that rebuilds the protective telomere caps on chromosomes — the basis for its marketing around aging and longevity.

What Research Actually Exists — and Its Central Limitation

Nearly every preclinical and clinical study behind Epithalon's reputation was conducted by the Khavinson group itself, without independent confirmation from unaffiliated labs — the single biggest limitation on how much confidence the evidence base can support. The foundational claim that Epithalon activates telomerase rests on a 2003 in-vitro study from that group reporting induced telomerase activity and telomere elongation in cultured human fibroblasts, without the kind of detailed statistical reporting (fold-changes, confidence intervals) that would let outside researchers fully evaluate it. A 2011 attempt by a separate US research group to replicate the telomerase-activation finding, using commercially synthesized AEDG, found no significant effect. More recent independent work (Al-Dulaimi et al., 2025) reported telomere-length increases through multiple pathways, offering some outside validation — but the overall picture remains a research area still working toward reliable independent replication, not settled science.

Current Legal & Regulatory Status

Epithalon has never been FDA-approved for any use in the United States. It was one of the peptides the FDA removed from its Category 2 "significant safety risk" compounding list effective April 22, 2026 — not approval, only a change in nomination status — and in the July 23–24, 2026 advisory committee meeting, the committee voted to recommend Epitalon for the compounding-permitted list, a recommendation that still requires formal FDA rulemaking (typically 8–24 months) before it would actually become legal to compound. Until then, it remains available only through unregulated research-chemical sellers.
