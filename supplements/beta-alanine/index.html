Beta-Alanine

Helps with short, high-intensity efforts, and comes with a harmless tingling side effect most people notice.

← Back to Supplements

Not medical advice — the tingling this supplement causes is expected and harmless, but talk to a doctor before starting if you're on other medications or have a health condition.

How It Works

Beta-alanine is an amino acid the body combines with histidine to form carnosine, which is stored in muscle and helps buffer the acid (hydrogen ions) that builds up during intense, sustained effort — the burn you feel during a hard set or sprint. Raising muscle carnosine through beta-alanine supplementation can delay that buildup slightly, which is why its benefits show up specifically in high-intensity efforts lasting roughly one to four minutes, not in short one-rep max lifts or long, steady endurance work.

The Research

Position stand: Trexler ET, Smith-Ryan AE, Stout JR, et al. "International Society of Sports Nutrition Position Stand: Beta-Alanine," Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition, 2015. Concludes 4–6g/day for at least 2–4 weeks reliably raises muscle carnosine content and improves performance in high-intensity exercise, most consistently for efforts in the 1–4 minute range; it's considered safe at recommended doses, with paresthesia (tingling) as its only notable side effect.

Read the position stand →

The tingling, explained: the tingling sensation (paresthesia) some people feel after taking beta-alanine comes from it activating a specific sensory nerve receptor in the skin. It's transient — typically starting within 10–20 minutes and resolving within about an hour — and dose dependent, meaning a larger single dose produces a stronger sensation. It's considered harmless, and the ISSN position stand notes it can be reduced by splitting the daily dose into smaller amounts (roughly 1.6g at a time) or using a sustained-release formulation, rather than taking the full daily dose at once.

Typical Dosing

4–6g/day, split into smaller doses of around 1.6g to minimize tingling, sustained for at least 2–4 weeks to build up muscle carnosine — this is a supplement that works cumulatively over time, not as an immediate pre-workout effect. Source: ISSN position stand, above.

Who Should Be Cautious

The tingling is harmless but can be uncomfortable for some people — splitting doses or using a sustained-release product reduces it. Beyond that, the ISSN position stand reports no serious safety concerns at recommended doses in the available research; as with any supplement, anyone with an existing health condition or on other medications should check with a doctor first.
